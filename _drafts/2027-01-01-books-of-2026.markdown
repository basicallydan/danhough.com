## Girt by David Hunt

Finished: Jan 2026

Quite funny and yet very informative account of the early days of colonisation in Australia, as well as the many, many examples of contact between outsiders and Aboriginal people before the British decided Australia belonged to the King. It focuses largely on human stories of people who had big impacts during early colonial days, and although it is critical of colonial narratives I think it could spend a little more time acknowledging the agency of Aboriginal Australians in this period. Nonetheless, I learned a lot and was frequently amused and I would thoroughly recommend this, as someone who has already read a fair bit on modern Australian history it is a lot more entertaining. Do take some bits with a pinch of salt, though.

## First Knowledges: Design - Building on Country by Alison Page & Paul Memmott

Finished: Feb 2026

A very interesting and enlightening discussion of ways in which Aboriginal design practices, mostly "classical" Aboriginal architecture and placemaking, worked before and during colonial times and how modern Australia could really benefit from the millennia-old wisdom of first peoples in tackling some of the challenges of the day. I learned an awful lot from this. Although a couple of times it did get a little dry the descriptions of how a lot of Aboriginal communities' social structures work and would've worked in the past are really interesting, and learning about the designs of tools and structures was great. But especially fascinating was the albiet only surface-level exploration (since it's covered in another book) of knowledge transfer across generations, as concepts like Songlines and the Dreaming really piqued my interest, so I look forward to learning more. Like with Dark Emu last year, this book is absolutely brilliant reading for anybody who wants to be inspired, dispel common myths and get a decent primer on the deep and very interesting truths about Aboriginal Australian cultures.
